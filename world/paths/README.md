# Shared world paths

`WorldPathNetwork` supplies one settlement-driven road network: routes between towns/cities, smaller homestead connections, village crossroads and squares, and front-door paths. Wilderness roads are no longer repeating mathematical curves. Deterministic settlement definitions identify destinations even before their scenes have loaded. Relative-neighbourhood links form the town backbone without connecting every town to every other town. Homes attach to their district's centre, or the nearest available centre in an empty district. Nearby bypass candidates are tried when terrain prevents a primary connection.

`TerrainRoadPlanner` searches the actual rendered height field, with distance and climbing costs. It samples the whole road width, rejects lava, waterfall gaps, camps, building footprints and city walls/moats, and limits road grades to 28 percent. The 64-metre search retries at 32 metres; a finer eight-metre edge validation rejects shortcuts missed by the coarse search. Safe corners are rounded. A cheap coastal preflight avoids expensive searches across broad seas. Searches have bounded expansions and a two-kilometre detour margin. Disconnected islands or terrain with no usable route remain disconnected rather than receiving broken roads.

Short crossings can span rivers or narrow dry ravines. Bridges have low-poly timber decks, approach ramps, rails and walking collision. They are owned by the chunk containing their midpoint and move with the floating origin. NPC route validation and ground support use the same bridge heights, so travellers can cross them. City roads join the outside end of the drawbridge; village roads join the existing street ends.

`PathMeshBuilder` renders the dirt lanes and softened shoulders clipped to world chunks and authoritative terrain triangles. `TerrainPathSampler` supplies the same actual-road wear and vegetation masks to terrain, grass, flowers, trees and rocks. Segment buckets avoid scanning whole kilometre-long polylines for every vegetation sample. Road travellers follow arcs extracted from these polylines. Empty road areas still support wilderness explorers.

Production road searches run through `GenerationScheduler` workers. Each worker owns isolated copies of the matching terrain/water samplers and footprint caches, returns plain road data, and supports cancellation during scene shutdown. Custom terrain/tests use the same planner cooperatively under the frame budget. Scene nodes and bridge collision are installed on the main thread. Rendering uses cached snapshots of each completed road and local settlement streets, independently of the larger regional search. Loaded settlements publish their local streets immediately; individual road completion refreshes affected path and vegetation chunks. Transparent shoulders derive opacity from the actual ready road rather than waiting for the regional cache. Near districts are planned first. Completed plans, including impossible connections, are cached under `user://world_roads/`; version and endpoint checks invalidate stale plans, and the cache is bounded to roughly 512 files. In-memory district, road and chunk caches are also bounded.

Base height generation and initial camp/building placement retain their old reservation/depression mask for world compatibility. Those reserved corridors no longer paint paths or clear vegetation. This keeps existing settlement positions and terrain elevations independent of road planning order, and prevents circular sampling.

Checks: `world/paths/tests/check_path_visibility.gd` verifies opaque, terrain-conforming streets while regional planning is deliberately blocked, late road refresh, grass masks and winding. `world/paths/tests/check_terrain_roads.gd` covers terrain/footprint detours, river/ravine crossings, bridge collision, ocean rejection, the settlement graph, sync/async parity, masks, road travellers and plan caching. `world/paths/tests/check_network.gd` checks production terrain and clipped meshes. `actors/npcs/villagers/tests/check_encounters.gd` exercises road parties and actual movement.

## Shore ferries

`FerrySampler` probes a deterministic 35 percent of 512-metre cells for paired landings. Both banks must have a gradient no greater than 22 percent across the approach, shoreline and submerged bank. Piers extend 12–40 metres into water at least a metre deep. Straight water lanes are limited to 768 metres and validated every four metres across the boat width; dry islands, shoals, large water-level changes, waterfalls, lava, ice-floe regions, camps and building footprints reject a candidate. No cliff-side docks or crossings through land are forced. These shore connections supplement the road network; they are not a global sea-routing solution.

`FerrySystem` searches on isolated terrain workers, caches cell results under `user://ferries/`, keeps at most four nearby crossings and cancels work during shutdown. Every pair of dock footprints must be at least 300 metres apart, regardless of bank or endpoint ordering; intersecting boat lanes are rejected too. Placement cache versions invalidate the older dense layout. Docks have batched timber planks, piles and solid deck collision. Boats are procedural faceted open wooden hulls with benches, gunwales, mast and sail. Clear short approaches connect to existing nearby road segments and share road/vegetation masks. Dock decks also reserve vegetation space for subsequently generated chunks.

Use **Interact (E / X)** at the end of a dock to immediately board a waiting wooden boat. Each bank maintains three moored boats; departing boats are replenished, and arrivals beyond that reserve are retired, keeping six idle boats plus occupied crossings. Boats sail independently, so travellers on either bank never need to wait for a return trip. They sail automatically at nine metres per second and disembark on the dry end of the opposite dock. Characters keep looking around while aboard; normal walking, swimming and gravity resume on arrival. Stable world coordinates handle floating-origin shifts. Each passenger's mid-crossing save records their own departure dock so loading never strands them over water.

A separate background journey job searches for safe land routes from each bank to actual towns or city entrances, favouring nearby cities. Only crossings with two checked settlement connections receive travelling passengers; isolated ferry docks stay available to the player without inventing dock-loop NPCs. Two travelling peasants use the normal NPC health, affection, animation and loot systems. Their complete itinerary walks from one settlement, boards the ferry, disembarks and continues to the other settlement, then pauses before returning. City journeys reach the exterior city entrance. Journey roads share the world path renderer, bridge support and cached vegetation masks, and late completion refreshes existing path and vegetation chunks. Following a passenger leads to a settlement rather than an endless series of boat trips. Combat interrupts boarding; a passenger killed aboard is returned to its departure dock before the corpse is created. `world/paths/tests/check_ferries.gd` checks shore rejection, collision, interaction ray, player/NPC travel, immediate boarding from both banks, safe save positions, rebasing and masks. Run with a graphical display to include the actual interaction-button test; the headless run exercises the same trip request directly because that driver cannot capture a mouse.
